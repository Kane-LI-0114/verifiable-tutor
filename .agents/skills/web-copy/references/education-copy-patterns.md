# Education-product copy patterns

Observed on the three captured sites (2026-11-13): Khanmigo, Brilliant, Tutor.com. These are
the patterns the genre rewards. Quotes are evidence; the project mapping is guidance, not
ready-made copy.

## The hero

Three working formulas, all in the captures:

1. **Role + availability** — "Khanmigo is your always-available teaching assistant."
   The product is a named role, and the promise is access, not magic.
2. **Possessive + scope** — "Your personal tutor for math and coding."
   Short, names the subject, no adjective doing the work.
3. **Outcome + audience** — "Online Tutoring That Accelerates Student Achievement."
   Weaker: the outcome is unattached to a mechanism, and "accelerates" is a claim no reader
   can check. Do not copy this shape.

The genre norm is the first two: name what the product is for the reader, in one line.

## Mechanism, not adjectives

The strongest lines explain how the thing behaves:

- "He asks guiding questions, sketches on your screen, and helps you work through each
  step." (Brilliant)
- "Khanmigo challenges you to think critically and solve problems without giving you direct
  answers." (Khanmigo)

Note that both describe a sequence of actions a user would recognize. Neither says
"intelligent" or "personalized".

## Differentiation by naming the failure mode

- "without giving you direct answers" (Khanmigo)
- "not a cheating machine" (Brilliant)

This works because the reader already knows the failure mode of the category (AI that just
hands over answers). If the product avoids that failure, say so plainly.

## Proof

- Numbers with a source and a unit: "4,000+ Expert Tutors in 250+ Subjects" (Tutor.com).
- Third-party validation: a named press quote, a rating body, a certification seal
  (Khanmigo, Brilliant).
- Outcome surveys with the method named: "Data from 2025 post-session learner surveys"
  (Tutor.com).

Never invent a number. This project's legitimate proof is its evaluation: representative
cases, recorded traces, checked citations. Use those, and say what they measured.

## Page structure

- Hero, then a section per audience (teachers / learners / parents), each with a short
  heading and two or three sentences.
- Feature blocks titled by what the user gets, not by the system component: "Track your
  progress and know what to practice next", not "Analytics module".
- FAQ written as real questions students ask, answered in plain prose. No marketing inside
  the answer.
- CTA verbs are plain: "Get started", "Sign up for free", "Learn more", "See how it works".
  Avoid "Unlock your potential".

## Weak patterns to avoid

- Institutional noun stacks: "actionable analytics to measure impact and continuously
  improve outcomes".
- Superlatives without evidence: "Unmatched tutor quality", "Powerful insights, real
  results".
- Three-item feature lists in every block.
- The word "AI" as the main claim. Every product in this category has AI; what differs is
  what it does with the course material.

## Mapping to Verifiable Tutor

- **Hero**: the differentiator is that answers are checkable against the course's own
  material. Candidate shapes (not final copy): "Answers from your course material, with the
  page and quote to check." / "以课程材料作答，每条引用都能核对。"
- **Mechanism**: retrieval from the course knowledge base → a quoted span → a check that the
  span exists on the page → the citation shown next to the answer. Say that in that order.
- **Disclosure**: the assignment policy decides what may be revealed; the system applies it
  in code. That is a real, unusual promise — state it without hedging.
- **Practice**: a reference answer that has been verified (compiled and tested where
  applicable), then grading against it with citations. "Verified" is the word that matters.
- **Audience split**: student (ask, practice, check), instructor/TA (deadlines, knowledge
  builds). Keep the instructor surface factual; it is a tool, not a sales page.
- **Microcopy** (Tier B): say what happened and what to do next. "无法连接后端" is better
  than "Oops! Something went wrong." No personality in error states.
