## Synthesis

**Norm (two or more sites)**

- Second person. The reader is "you"; the product is a named role ("teaching assistant",
  "personal tutor").
- Headlines are short and name a role, a subject, or a task. No abstract nouns.
- Mechanism is described as a sequence of concrete actions: ask, sketch, guide, work
  through, check.
- Differentiation names the category's failure mode and says the product avoids it:
  "without giving you direct answers", "not a cheating machine".
- Proof is specific when present: a number with a unit, a named rating, a survey with a
  method.

**Outliers**

- Tutor.com speaks to institutions ("Partner with us", "your institution"), which fits its
  B2B model; not the norm for a student-facing product.
- Brilliant's playful parent line ("Turn homework into high fives") is one site's voice, not
  the genre's.

**Weak lines — negative examples**

- "Online Tutoring That Accelerates Student Achievement" — outcome with no mechanism.
- "Powerful insights, real results" / "Unmatched tutor quality" — superlatives replacing
  evidence.
- "Real-time data and actionable analytics to measure impact and continuously improve
  outcomes" — noun stack a reader cannot picture.

**Lexicon**

tutor, teaching assistant, guided questions, step by step, work through, practice, mastery,
assessment, feedback, progress, course material, homework, test prep.

## Implications for Verifiable Tutor

- Lead with the mechanism this product actually has: answers cite a page and a quoted span,
  and the span is checked against the page text. That is stronger than "AI-powered".
- Name what the product will not do (invent an answer, reveal what the assignment policy
  forbids). The genre rewards that honesty.
- Use only real proof: evaluation cases, recorded traces, checked citations. No invented
  student counts.
- Chinese: direct address, verb first, no 赋能/助力/开启学习之旅. The existing line
  "以课程材料为依据、引用可核查的答疑。" is the register to match.
